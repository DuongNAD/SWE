I. CASE STUDY
"CityCare Hospital" is upgrading its legacy paper records to a digital "Patient Portal & Clinic
Management System* (PPCMS). Patients utilize the portal to book appointments, view their
digital prescriptions, and download lab results. If a patient is unable to attend, they can cancel
their appointment; if done within 24 hours of the slot, the system automatically offers the
opening to the next patient on a "Priority Waiting List." Doctors use the system to review patient
medical histories, record consultation notes, and issue electronic prescriptions directly to the
hospital pharmacy. Nurses are responsible for checking patients in upon arrival, recording vital
signs (weight, blood pressure), and updating the room occupancy status. Medical Records
Officers manage the archiving of sensitive data and ensure all records comply with national
health privacy laws. An external Insurance Provider System is linked to the PPCMS to verify
coverage and process billing claims automatically after a consultation.
The PPCMS must be highly secure, utilizing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all staff
logins. The system must support at least 200 concurrent medical staff users without
performance degradation. As per legal requirements, the system must maintain a full audit log
of every user who accesses a patient's file. The interface must be optimized for tablets, as
doctors and nurses move frequently between wards.

Management System" (PPCMS). Patients utilize the portal to book appointments, view their

hospital pharmacy. Nurses are responsible for checking patients in upon arrival, recording vital

health privacy laws. An external Insurance Provider System is linked to the PPCMS to verify

logins. The system must support at least 200 concurrent medical staff users without

I. CASE STUDY
"CityCare Hospital" is upgrading its legacy paper records to a digital "Patient Portal & Clinic
Management System" (PPCMS). Patients utilize the portal to book appointments, view their
digital prescriptions, and download lab results. If a patient is unable to attend, they can cancel
their appointment; if done within 24 hours of the slot, the system automatically offers the
opening to the next patient on a "Priority Waiting List." Doctors use the system to review patient
medical histories, record consultation notes, and issue electronic prescriptions directly to the
hospital pharmacy. Nurses are responsible for checking patients in upon arrival, recording vital
signs (weight, blood pressure), and updating the room occupancy status. Medical Records
Officers manage the archiving of sensitive data and ensure all records comply with national
health privacy laws. An external Insurance Provider System is linked to the PPCMS to verify
coverage and process billing claims automatically after a consultation.
The PPCMS must be highly secure, utilizing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all staff
logins. The system must support at least 200 concurrent medical staff users without
performance degradation. As per legal requirements, the system must maintain a full audit log
of every user who accesses a patient's file. The interface must be optimized for tablets, as
doctors and nurses move frequently between wards.

PRACTICE TEST 2: HEALTHCARE MANAGEMENT
SYSTEM
I. CASE STUDY
"CityCare Hospital" is upgrading its legacy paper records to a digital "Patient Portal & Clinic
Management System" (PPCMS). Patients utilize the portal to book appointments, view their
digital prescriptions, and download lab results. If a patient is unable to attend, they can cancel
their appointment; if done within 24 hours of the slot, the system automatically offers the
opening to the next patient on a "Priority Waiting List." Doctors use the system to review patient
medical histories, record consultation notes, and issue electronic prescriptions directly to the
hospital pharmacy. Nurses are responsible for checking patients in upon arrival, recording vital
signs (weight, blood pressure), and updating the room occupancy status. Medical Records
Officers manage the archiving of sensitive data and ensure all records comply with national
health privacy laws. An external Insurance Provider System is linked to the PPCMS to verify
coverage and process billing claims automatically after a consultation.
The PPCMS must be highly secure, utilizing Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all staff
logins. The system must support at least 200 concurrent medical staff users without
performance degradation. As per legal requirements, the system must maintain a full audit log
of every user who accesses a patient's file. The interface must be optimized for tablets, as
doctors and nurses move frequently between wards.
