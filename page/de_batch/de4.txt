I Case Study
MedCare Solutions (MCS) is a mid-sized healthcare technology firm based in Vietnam whose mission is to enhance patient care through digital innovation. The company
serves public and private medical clinics across the country. Seeing the rising demand for efficient healthcare management tools, MCS's board of directors (BOD) has
decided to build a comprehensive Clinic Management System (CMS) to modernize clinic operations and completely replace paper-based workflows.
Through a single centralized platform, the CMS connects patients, doctors, nurses, receptionists, pharmacists, and administrators. When a patient arrives, a receptionist —
or the patient themselves — creates a digital profile storing demographics, health insurance, allergies, and full medical history. Appointments can be booked online or at
the front desk with real-time doctor availability, automated SMS/email reminders, and dynamic queue management with live wait-time estimates on the day of the visit.
During the consultation, nurses record vital signs (BP, temperature, HR, SpO2) while doctors enter ICD-10 diagnoses and treatment plans via structured templates; all
entries are time-stamped and attributed to the responsible clinician. Lab and imaging orders (X-ray, ultrasound, MRI) are raised electronically from the EMR, results are
auto-linked to the patient record, and critical values trigger immediate alerts. Doctors issue e-prescriptions with automatic drug-drug interaction and allergy checks;
prescriptions flow to the pharmacy for dispensing with real-time inventory updates and low-stock alerts. Administrators manage user roles (RBAC), schedules, and fee
structures, and can view KPI dashboards covering patient volume, revenue, and staff productivity.
In addition to the above functional requirements, the system must allow staff users to sign in with MCS's corporate email account via the MCS Identity Platform (Single
Sign-On). The CMS must be intuitive and easy to use, even for clinical staff with limited technical experience. The interface must be clean and consistent, with clearly
labeled navigation for high-frequency tasks such as patient lookup, appointment creation, and prescription entry. Color coding must be used meaningfully - green to
confirm actions, red for errors or critical alerts — so staff can act quickly without ambiguity.
The system must cope with peak transaction volumes without performance degradation, especially during morning rush hours when patient check-ins, consultations, and
lab requests happen simultaneously. Under normal operating load, the system shall respond to user interactions (page loading, form submission, search) within 2 seconds.
The system shall also scale horizontally to support a growing number of patients, clinics, and staff users without service interruption.
The CMS is a green-field project with evolving requirements; MCS's IT department leads the development with 3-4 experienced developers and clinical representatives,
targeting the first release in 3 months and full completion within 9 months.
